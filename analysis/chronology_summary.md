## Precursor Languages and Early Development (1960-1969)

### The JOSS Lineage
- **1963**: JOSS (JOHNNIAC Open Shop System) first implemented at RAND Corporation - First interactive time-sharing language
- **1964**: TELCOMP development begins at BBN (Bolt, Beranek and Newman)
- **1965**: TELCOMP first revenue in October 1965; commercial time-sharing service in Boston area

### MUMPS Birth at MGH
- **1964**: Neil Pappalardo begins work on hospital information systems while at MIT
- **1966**: MUMPS development begins at Massachusetts General Hospital by Neil Pappalardo, Robert Greenes, and Curt Marble in Dr. Octo Barnett's lab
- **1966**: Original MUMPS system built on DEC PDP-7 computer
- **1966**: MEDITECH (Medical Information Technology, Inc.) founded by A. Neil Pappalardo
- **1967**: MUMPS project funded by NIH; serious implementation begins
- **1967**: STRINGCOMP developed at BBN with extended string handling (influenced MUMPS database integration)
- **1967**: Octo Barnett and Neil Pappalardo obtain backward-compatible PDP-9; MUMPS used in admissions and laboratory test reporting
- **1967**: MEDITECH introduces first hospital information system products
- **1968**: Early MUMPS use in experimental clinical progress note entry system
- **1968**: Early MUMPS use in radiology report entry system
- **1968**: Computer Stored Ambulatory Record (COSTAR) developed at MGH using MUMPS
- **1969**: MIIS (Meditech Interpretive Information System) variant of MUMPS developed

**Key Insight**: MUMPS emerged from JOSS → TELCOMP → STRINGCOMP lineage, with direct development at MGH starting in 1966.

## Standards and Early Maturation (1970-1979)

### Standards Development
- **1977**: First ANSI standard for MUMPS Programming Language (X11.1-1977)
- **1978**: InterSystems Corporation founded by Terry Ragon
- **1978**: ONTAP: Online Training and Practice Manual published by Markey & Atherton (Syracuse University) - describes citation pearl growing methodology
- **1978**: Markey & Cochrane publish on citation pearl growing approach
- **1979**: Epic Systems founded by Judith Faulkner

### Government and Early Applications
- **1970**: TELCOMP service sold to On-Line Systems, Inc. (OLS)
- **1970**: Antrim Corporation founded (later acquired by Sunquest in 1996)
- **1972**: DHCP (Decentralized Hospital Computer Program) developed at VA - precursor to VistA
- **1974**: TELCOMP in use until at least 1974
- **1975**: VA's DHCP system begins deployment across veterans hospitals
- **1977**: DHCP renamed to VistA (Veterans Health Information Systems and Technology Architecture)

**Key Insight**: First ANSI standard established in 1977, while VA's VistA system began taking shape as a major MUMPS deployment.

## Commercialization and Industry Adoption (1980-1989)

### Vendor Ecosystem
- **1980**: InterSystems releases first commercial products
- **1981**: Design of a MUMPS interpreter by F.M. Brown (published in Software: Practice and Experience)
- **1981**: Micro MUMPS: An interactive database language for micro-computers by Watanabe et al.
- **1982**: InterSystems Caché first released
- **1982**: Computerized administrative monitoring system for interns and residents
- **1982**: Hawkins & Wagers publish "Online Bibliographic Search Strategy Development" describing citation pearl growing methodology
- **1984**: MEDITECH expands product line with MAGIC language

### Healthcare Expansion
- **1980**: VA VistA deployment expands across veterans hospitals
- **1982**: A Computerized Perinatal Data System using Meditech Interpretive Information System (MIIS) version of MUMPS

### Standards Evolution
- **1980**: Second ANSI standard for MUMPS Programming Language (X11.1-1980)
- **1984**: Third ANSI standard for MUMPS Programming Language (X11.1-1984)
- **1984**: ANSI standard for MUMPS-GKS Binding (X11.3-1984)

**Key Insight**: Commercial vendors (InterSystems, MEDITECH) emerge; MUMPS gains traction in healthcare with standardized implementations.

## Expansion and Standardization (1990-1999)

### International Standards
- **1990**: Fourth ANSI standard for MUMPS Programming Language (X11.1-1990)
- **1990**: ISO adoption of MUMPS standard (ISO 11756:1992)
- **1992**: ISO 11756:1992 MUMPS Programming Language standard published
- **1994**: ANSI standard for MUMPS-GKS Binding (X11.3-1994)
- **1995**: Fifth ANSI standard for MUMPS Programming Language (X11.1-1995)
- **1995**: ANSI standard for Open MUMPS Interconnect (X11.2-1995)
- **1995**: ANSI standard for MUMPS Windowing API (X11.6-1995)
- **1999**: ISO 11756:1999 MUMPS Programming Language standard published
- **1999**: ISO/IEC 15851:1999 Open MUMPS Interconnect standard published
- **1999**: ISO/IEC 15852:1999 MUMPS Windowing API standard published

### Financial Sector Adoption
- **1990**: Bank of England adopts MUMPS-based systems for core banking operations
- **1990**: Barclays Bank implements MUMPS-based core banking systems
- **1990**: Multiple US and UK credit unions adopt MUMPS-based systems

### Vendor Growth
- **1990**: VistA achieves widespread deployment across all VA hospitals
- **1991**: MEDITECH Client/Server for Windows released
- **1995**: VA VistA wins Computerworld Smithsonian Award for best use of Information Technology in Medicine
- **1995**: Incorporation of the Arden Syntax within reimplementation of closed-loop decision support system; conversion to M (MUMPS) code
- **1996**: Sunquest Information Systems acquires Antrim Corporation
- **1996**: Large companies using MUMPS include Epic, MEDITECH, GE Healthcare, AmeriPath, Care Centric, Allscripts, Coventry Health Care, Sunquest
- **1997**: MEDITECH Web-based products introduced
- **1997**: InterSystems releases Caché database system
- **1998**: OCLC drops LLS development, adopts Lister Hill ILS
- **1998**: InterSystems acquires Micronetics Design Corporation (MSM)
- **1998**: InterSystems becomes dominant M technology vendor
- **1999**: NLM halts public access to ELHILL retrieval system
- **1999**: Farewell to ELHILL: 28 years of MEDLINE support

**Key Insight**: ISO standardization in 1992; MUMPS expands beyond healthcare into financial services; VistA achieves widespread adoption.

## Modern Era and Open Source (2000-2009)

### Open Source and New Platforms
- **2000**: GT.M for Linux released on November 7, 2000 by Fidelity National Information Services
- **2000**: InterSystems HealthShare platform released
- **2000**: GT.M for Linux released by FIS (open source)
- **2000**: InterSystems launches Ensemble integration platform
- **2001**: Antrim Corporation (Sunquest) code base used by reference laboratories including Quest Diagnostics, Dynacare, AmeriPath, DASA
- **2001**: Sunquest acquired by Misys plc
- **2002**: FIS acquires Sanchez Computer Associates (GT.M owner)
- **2005**: YottaDB forked from GT.M as open source implementation
- **2005**: VA VistA wins Innovations in American Government Award presented by Harvard Kennedy School
- **2005**: GT.M and Caché become the most popular MUMPS implementations
- **2006**: InterSystems launches HealthShare platform
- **2006**: UC Davis Veterinary Medical Teaching Hospital adapts VistA to create VMACS (Veterinary Medical Application System)
- **2006**: UC Davis VMTH M2Web demonstration (December 2008)
- **2007**: Sunquest Diagnostic Information Systems acquired by Misys Healthcare Systems Division
- **2007**: Medsphere Systems founded, offering OpenVistA (open-source VistA)
- **2008**: GT.M open source release (AGPL-3.0 license)
- **2008**: Sunquest emerges from Misys shadow as independent company
- **2008**: MEDITECH Client/Server 5.x released

### Continued Industry Adoption
- **2004**: Epic Systems exceeds 1,000 customer sites
- **2005**: ISO reaffirms MUMPS standards (11756, 15851, 15852)
- **2009**: MEDITECH Expanse (web-based EHR) development begins

**Key Insight**: Open source implementations emerge (GT.M, YottaDB); VistA adoption expands to international and veterinary applications.
